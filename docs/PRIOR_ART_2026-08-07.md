## Verdict on the "silent failure" framing: PARTIALLY TAKEN

### The nearest competitor — read this first

**arXiv:2607.02586, "Auditing the Audit: Five Failure Modes in Benchmark-Validity
Audits"** (Jul 1, 2026). Published five weeks ago, one subfield over. It defines
*silent* exactly as we did — invisible in the reported numbers — runs a
self-audit over 2 models × 5 benchmarks, defines five failure classes, and ships
a withholding-and-disclosure protocol with a per-bug chronology template. It
also pre-empts the generalizability objection with explicit inclusion criteria
(silent / realized-not-hypothesised / gateable).

**We cannot introduce the concept. We can instantiate it.** Their excluded set
names no shape/broadcast failure, no wrong-artifact-loaded failure, no seeding,
no model-version pinning. Their extraction-artefact class does collide with our
judge-extraction leg.

**Consequence:** cite it in the first paragraph and position our contribution as
*the failure classes their case study could not realize, in the subfield they did
not cover.* Presenting silent-failure framing as new would read as unaware of
related work.

### Also taken

  cannot see." **One of its authors co-organizes the Interpretability for

- **arXiv:2607.12166** — reproducible audit of SAE features; establishes

- "Steering is unreliable" — thoroughly occupied: arXiv:2407.12404 (Tan et al.),
  arXiv:2504.04635 (Da Silva et al., ACL 2025, 36 models), arXiv:2602.17881,
  arXiv:2603.24543. All frame their findings as **genuine limits, not
  implementation issues** — which is the distinction our claim turns on.
- Silent-bug taxonomy in ML generally: arXiv:2112.13314 (Keras/TensorFlow, EMSE
  2024), SANER 2024 on PyTorch user code, arXiv:2604.08720 (19.2% of
  high-priority PyTorch issues are silent correctness bugs).
- Judge-extraction distortion: arXiv:2503.14996 ("Right Answer, Wrong Score",
  Findings of ACL 2025). Unpinned aliases: arXiv:2604.25580, arXiv:2604.07530.
- The genre's gold standard: Kapoor & Narayanan, leakage and the reproducibility
  crisis (arXiv:2207.07048, *Patterns* 2023) — 17 fields, 329 papers, taxonomy,
  checklist. **That structure is what our paper will be measured against.**

### Genuinely open

1. **Silent broadcast** — shapes that are legal but semantically wrong. Argued
   rhetorically by einops and the named-tensors line; **never measured.** Shape-
   *mismatch* tooling is saturated (PyTea, ShapeFlow); shape-compatible-but-wrong
   is not.
2. **Content-addressed tensor-payload hashing as a scientific-validity control**
   rather than a tamper/corruption control.
3. **The claim that published steering nulls may be bug artifacts.** No paper in
   ML makes this, in any subfield. It is also the claim we currently have **zero
   evidence for** — the literature's bias runs the other way (leakage inflates
   *positives*). Asserting it without measurement reads as slander-by-conjecture,
   and non-identifiability (arXiv:2602.06801) already supplies a principled
   non-accusatory explanation for steering nulls.

## Why the forensic-case-study framing fails on its own

Stated as a reviewer would: Kapoor & Narayanan had 329 papers; arXiv:2602.18458
had 30 mech-interp outputs; we have **one unpublished four-month student
project.** The one-line rejection writes itself — *a broken unpublished project
produced broken numbers; this is not a finding about the literature.* Secondary
objections: the bugs look elementary and the fix is one line; detectors built for
failures we already knew we had have no held-out validation; and self-forensics
has a trust problem (who audits the auditors).

**The interestingness is not the bug. It is that the bug produced a coherent
result table that survived a year.** Survivability has to be the object of study.

## The fix, in priority order

1. **The injected-fault study.** Take a working pipeline, inject each failure
   class one at a time, show the resulting tables are indistinguishable by
   inspection from real effects and real nulls, and quantify: what fraction of
   cells change, what effect size each fault manufactures, whether standard
   reporting would catch it. Converts "we had bugs" into a **measured
   susceptibility profile**, and makes the nulls claim testable rather than
   insinuated. Structural precedent to cite and differentiate from:
   arXiv:2604.16286 (sabotaged ML codebases) — theirs is adversarial, ours is
   silent-by-construction.
2. **Run the detectors over public steering repos** and report hit rate *and*
   false-positive rate. Reachable corpus: `steering-vectors`, `repeng`, Dialz
   (arXiv:2505.06262), AI Steerability 360, `dtch1997/steering-bench`,
   `andyrdt/refusal_direction`, `EasyEdit2`. **Even N=20 with zero hits is
   publishable** — "the ecosystem is cleaner than our archive, and here is why."
3. **Instantiate arXiv:2607.02586's protocol rather than reinventing it** —
   adopt their inclusion criteria and chronology template, and position against
   them. Turns the biggest novelty threat into framing scaffold.

The archive then stops being the paper and becomes the **existence proof** that
motivates the study: one paragraph and one figure.

## Venue conflict — a real decision, not a preference

| venue | deadline | format | negative-results language |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interpretability for Discovery @ NeurIPS 2026** (Atlanta) | **Aug 29 AoE** | 5pp, non-archival, double-blind | **Explicit.** "Failure cases and negative results are welcome… careful studies of unsuccessful methods, misleading interpretations, failed validation." Named topic bucket. Reviewers told to weigh reproducibility. |
| **Interpretability as a Science @ NeurIPS 2026** (Sydney) | **Aug 28** | ≤5pp short / ≤9pp long, non-archival | No "negative results" wording, but topics include *"falsifiability and experimental designs that distinguish mechanisms from artifacts"* and *"measurement validity, identifiability, and evaluation design."* |

**⚠️ Interpretability as a Science forbids concurrent submission to any other
NeurIPS 2026 workshop. These are mutually exclusive. The team must choose one.**

On fit: Interp4Discovery's CFP explicitly invites what we would be submitting.
Interpretability-as-a-Science's *topic list* is closer to the actual content
(artifacts vs mechanisms, measurement validity) and allows a 9-page long paper,
which suits a study with a susceptibility table plus controls. Deadline is one
day earlier.

**Archival credit lives elsewhere.** Both are non-archival. BlackboxNLP has a
purpose-built "Reproducibility and Reliability in Interpretability Analyses"
track whose CFP language is the closest structural match in existence — but 2026
submission has closed; target 2027. TMLR (rolling, archival, no novelty bar,
scope explicitly includes reproducibility studies) feeds MLRC, now a NeurIPS
track.
