\section{Abstention dominates both the benchmark and the representation}
\label{sec:run3}

Section~\ref{sec:intro} argued that bias benchmarks cannot separate a model that
answers fairly from one that evades. We test that claim directly, and find that
it holds twice over: evasion is the majority behaviour on BBQ, and it is also
the dominant component of any direction extracted from a BBQ-derived bias
contrast. The second finding is the stronger one, because it means abstention
is not merely miscounted at evaluation time --- it structurally displaces the
concept a practitioner believes they are steering.

\subsection{Method}
\label{sec:run3:method}

\paragraph{Behavioural contrast.}
Prior work fits bias directions to a \emph{margin} computed over answer-option
likelihoods, which risks fitting the scoring function rather than the behaviour.
We instead define the contrast on what the model actually generates. For a
category $C$,
%
\begin{equation}
V_C \;=\; \mathbb{E}\big[h \,\big|\, \text{model named the stereotyped referent}\big]
        \;-\; \mathbb{E}\big[h \,\big|\, \text{model declined to name anyone}\big],
\label{eq:contrast}
\end{equation}
%
where $h$ is the residual stream captured at \texttt{hook\_resid\_pre} across all
layers at the final prompt token. No option scoring enters
Equation~\ref{eq:contrast}, so $V_C$ cannot be an artifact of the option-scoring
function.

\paragraph{Data and generation.}
We use all ten BBQ categories~\citep{parrish2022bbq}, sampling 400 ambiguous
items per category (232 for Sexual\_orientation, the full pool) and 1{,}000
disambiguated items per model, for 4{,}832 generations per model and 9{,}664 in
total across two open-weight instruction-tuned models: \texttt{01-ai/Yi-6B-Chat}
($d_{\mathrm{model}}{=}4096$, 32 layers) and \texttt{Qwen/Qwen1.5-14B-Chat}
($5120$, 40 layers). Prompts are $\text{context} + \text{question}$ with the
answer options withheld; displaying them makes the measured preference a
function of list position rather than content.
% SOURCE: results/run3/{yi-6b,qwen-14b}/responses.jsonl = 4832 lines each;
% per_category.*.n_total in report_behavioural.json sums to 3832 ambiguous.

\paragraph{Labelling and its qualification.}
A judge (\texttt{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18}) assigns each completion to one of four
buckets: stance toward the stereotyped referent, refusal, distractor, or
unparsable. Because a position-biased judge would write a presentation-order
error directly into the extraction arms, we relabel every ambiguous completion
with the options presented in reversed order and require $\geq 95\%$ agreement
on the resulting bucket label over a 200-item sample. The judge attains $0.990$
(Yi-6B) and $1.000$ (Qwen1.5-14B) with zero format failures. We additionally
require the judge to be non-degenerate --- to emit more than one distinct verdict
--- because an earlier version returned \textsc{refusal} to every input and would
have scored perfect order agreement while being entirely uninformative.
% SOURCE: results/run3/{model}/judge_qualification.json: order_agreement,
% n_format_failures, n_distinct_verdicts (5 and 4), qualified=true.
% NOTE: order_agreement_raw_option is lower (0.875 / 0.820). The gating figure
% is the bucket label. Say which you used.

\paragraph{Reproducibility criterion.}
A direction is only interpretable if it is stable. We split each category's
items into halves stratified by bucket, extract independently from each half,
and record the norm-weighted mean per-layer cosine between the two. Over 400
such splits we report the mean and a percentile bootstrap CI; we call this the
\emph{extraction floor}. The decision rule is threshold-free: a direction
reproduces iff the lower bound of its floor exceeds the upper bound of a
label-shuffled control run through the identical estimator. Categories with
fewer than $32$ items in either arm are marked \textsc{untestable} and no floor
is computed --- a distinction we hold to throughout, since ``no contrast was
available to split'' is not evidence that no direction exists.

\paragraph{Positive control.}
To separate instrument failure from construct absence, we run a contrast that is
unambiguously present --- topic identity between two categories --- through the
identical estimator, capture site and split-half procedure at matched $n$. On
\texttt{Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat} with 200 items per arm and 100 splits, this
reproduces at $0.876$, $0.923$ and $0.934$ for three category pairs. The
pipeline therefore recovers a real direction; any category that fails does so
for another reason.
% SOURCE: results/run3/_control_qwen-1.8b/positive_control.json, pairs.*.floor.mean

\paragraph{Refusal reference.}
Equation~\ref{eq:contrast} subtracts a refusal arm, which injects a refusal
signature into every $V_C$ by construction. To measure how much, we need a
reference refusal direction obtained \emph{without} circularity. We extract it
from the \emph{disambiguated} arm, where the context names the referent, so
declining reflects non-engagement rather than epistemic caution about a
stereotype. Pooling the ambiguous arm's refusal buckets across categories would
instead yield approximately the mean of the $V_C$, and orthogonalising five
vectors against their own mean removes whatever they share regardless of what it
is --- a construction that returns ``collapse'' by arithmetic. Each
orthogonalisation is read against removal of a norm- and covariance-matched
random direction, since removing \emph{any} direction changes a vector somewhat.
% SOURCE: report_behavioural.json -> refusal_direction.source = "answerable_arm";
% refusal_proxy_validation.note records the circularity this avoids.

\paragraph{Causal arm.}
We inject $\pm V_C$ uniformly at all layers at
$\alpha \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.50, 1.00\}$ of the per-layer mean residual
norm, and score 200 held-out items per category --- items excluded from
extraction --- with a judge-free likelihood margin,
$\log P(\text{stereotyped}) - \log P(\text{other named})$, both terms mean
log-probability per token so option lengths cancel. The model generates nothing,
so no completion needs parsing and no judge version enters the causal claim. An
accuracy control on disambiguated items is scored at every dose, so a direction
that merely damages the model is visible as accuracy collapse rather than as a
clean effect.

\subsection{Evasion is the majority behaviour, and it is not uniformly distributed}
\label{sec:run3:buckets}

declines on $84.3$--$100\%$ of ambiguous items; on Race\_x\_gender it produced
\emph{zero} stance-taking responses in 400 generations. Yi-6B declines less

\label{sec:run3:floors}

\subsection{The shared structure across bias categories is largely refusal}
\label{sec:run3:decoupling}

Directions for different bias categories are strongly similar --- median
off-diagonal cosine $0.650$ (Yi-6B, five categories, ten pairs) and $0.620$
(Qwen1.5-14B), against permutation nulls with medians $-0.005$ and $0.009$ and
$p = 0.001$ in both ($1{,}000$ permutations). Read naively this suggests a common
bias mechanism. It does not survive the control.

The concern is geometric rather than statistical. Every $V_C$ subtracts a
refusal arm, and cosine measures angle from the origin rather than distance
between points: a shared additive component leaves pairwise \emph{differences}
untouched while driving pairwise \emph{angles} arbitrarily close. Two directions
with nothing in common can be pushed to cosine $0.9$ by a sufficiently large
shared term.

Table~\ref{tab:run3refusal} quantifies it. Each individual direction carries
$31.7$--$56.4\%$ of its variance along the refusal reference (mean $49\%$ across
Yi-6B's five categories), without being dominated by it --- no category exceeds
the indistinguishability ceiling set by how well each direction is itself
measured. At the aggregate level, orthogonalising the refusal reference away
removes $50.7\%$ of Yi-6B's shared cross-category structure, taking the median
off-diagonal from $0.650$ to $0.320$, whereas removing a norm- and
covariance-matched random direction removes only $4.8\%$. The removal is
targeted, not mechanical.

The two models return opposite verdicts against a retention bar of $0.500$ fixed
in advance, and both sit within $0.09$ of it. We report this rather than
resolve it, with one asymmetry noted: Qwen1.5-14B has only two testable
categories, so its ``median off-diagonal'' is a single pair with a degenerate
confidence interval, while Yi-6B's rests on ten. Only one of the two
measurements has power. We also note that orthogonalisation is a lower bound on
contamination, since the reference is itself measured only to its own floor
($0.821$), so the true share may exceed the $50.7\%$ reported --- an error that
runs toward the refusal explanation rather than away from it. Finally, PCA
cannot adjudicate this question and we do not use it to: Yi-6B's explained
variance ratios are $0.356 / 0.282 / 0.193 / 0.169$, with no dominant component,
and a dominant component would in any case have been equally consistent with one
shared mechanism and one shared artifact.

\subsection{Uniform all-layer steering has no operating window}
\label{sec:run3:steering}

At every dose that preserves task performance ($\alpha \leq 0.10$), no injected
direction produced a margin shift whose confidence interval excludes zero, in
either model, in any of the seven testable cells. The two positive-arm shifts
that do clear zero --- Yi-6B Physical\_appearance at $\alpha = 0.5$ and $1.0$,
$-0.455$ and $-0.449$ --- occur where disambiguated-item accuracy falls from
$0.617$ to $0.317$, and a matched random direction at the same doses produces
$-0.542$ and $-0.577$: larger than the fitted direction. In Yi-6B
Disability\_status the random control clears zero at four of five doses while the
fitted direction clears it at none.

The upper doses do not steer; they destroy. In the judged generation arm, at
$\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0\}$ across both categories and all four arms --- positive,
negative, random-control and task-control --- $100\%$ of $3{,}200$ completions
were unparsable, with unsteered baselines in the same run healthy at $68.7\%$ and
$85.3\%$ accuracy. The collapse is caused by the dose and is indifferent to
which direction is injected.
% SOURCE: report_steering_likelihood.json -> cells.{cat}.doses.{alpha};
% qwen-14b/report_steering.json -> cells.*.doses.*.unknown_rate = 1.000 in all
% four arms at alpha 0.5 and 1.0; baselines accuracy_vs_bbq_label 0.687 / 0.853.

This is the null that motivates Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}. Applying a direction
at uniform strength across all layers leaves no dose at which the model is both
intact and moved: below $\alpha = 0.35$ nothing happens, at and above it nothing
survives. Adaptive and linearly scaled steering are not refinements of a working
method --- they are responses to the absence of a working uniform one, and the
result above is the control against which any adaptive gain should be read.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:run3:limits}

These results rest on two models rather than the four planned, so generality
across families and scales is untested, and the two disagree on the one contested
result. Because no race-related category was testable in either model, nothing
here bears on race; those cells are \textsc{untestable}, and reading them as
negative findings would repeat the confound the paper is about. The Yi-6B floors
are estimator-sensitive --- an unweighted median moves Religion from $0.727$ to
$0.501$ --- and its testable buckets are drawn from roughly a third of collected
items, imposing a selection effect of unknown direction on which completions
parse. The refusal proxy is markedly better in one model than the other
($0.954$ versus $0.768$ alignment against ceiling), and the orthogonalisation is
a lower bound in both. The causal metric scores a disposition over answer
options rather than generated behaviour, so its null does not fully close the
behavioural question; it was adopted in place of judged generation partly because
the upper doses leave nothing to judge, and partly under an API rate limit. The
raw completions at the collapsing doses were not preserved, so the unparsable
rate is documented but the emitted text is unrecoverable. Finally, all results
are at a single capture site and token position.
