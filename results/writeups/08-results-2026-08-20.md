# Experiment 1 — results, night of 2026-08-20

Model: `qwen-1.8b` (Qwen/Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat), Lambda A100-40GB.
Run dir on box: `runs/full_qwen18`. All numbers below are from committed
artifacts, not from memory.

## The headline

**The measurement works. The thing we set out to measure is not there at this
model scale.**

That is a different sentence from "bias has no subtypes", and the difference is
the whole result. We can say it because of one control.

## What was established, in order

### 1. The scorer is valid — positive control on disambiguated items

On *disambiguated* BBQ items the context does identify the right person, so
there is a correct answer. Scoring each option's likelihood with **no option
list in the prompt**, qwen-1.8b picks it well above the 1/3 chance rate:

| category | accuracy | z |
|---|---|---|
| Religion | 64.0% | +8.0 |
| Race_x_SES | 62.0% | +7.4 |
| Race_ethnicity | 61.3% | +7.3 |
| Race_x_gender | 60.7% | +7.1 |
| Gender_identity | 58.7% | +6.6 |
| Nationality | 56.0% | +5.9 |
| Age | 55.3% | +5.7 |
| Sexual_orientation | 55.3% | +5.7 |
| **Physical_appearance** | **49.3%** | +4.2 → **dropped** |
| **Disability_status** | **44.7%** | +2.9 → **dropped** |

Gate was ≥50% accuracy and z≥3, fixed before any category ran. 8 of 10 pass.

Worth noting: Physical_appearance was the *only* category that showed real
stereotype preference under the earlier generation-based method (z=+3.1). It is
dropped here because the model cannot reliably identify the right person even
when told. A "bias signal" from a category the model does not comprehend would
have been an artifact.

### 2. The extraction machinery works — positive control on topic identity

Extract a direction for **topic** (Race_ethnicity prompts vs Gender_identity
prompts) through the *identical* capture site, mean-difference and split-half
machinery, at matched n:

| topic contrast | n | floor q05 | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| Race_ethnicity vs Gender_identity | 320 | **0.882** | 0.899 |
| Religion vs Age | 320 | **0.893** | 0.912 |
| Nationality vs Sexual_orientation | 320 | **0.871** | 0.906 |

Re-extracting the same topic contrast from random halves recovers the same
direction at cosine ~0.88. **The residual stream at the final prompt token
cleanly encodes what a question is about, and our pipeline recovers it.**

### 3. The bias-margin directions do not reproduce

Same machinery, same n, same everything — but contrasting the top and bottom
quintile of the *stereotype margin* within a category:

| category | n | floor q05 | median |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | 320 | 0.423 | 0.478 |
| Race_x_gender | 320 | 0.138 | 0.380 |
| Nationality | 320 | 0.057 | 0.359 |
| Race_x_SES | 320 | 0.017 | 0.239 |
| Religion | 240 | −0.008 | 0.139 |
| Gender_identity | 318 | −0.072 | 0.065 |
| Race_ethnicity | 320 | −0.115 | 0.048 |
| Sexual_orientation | 172 | −0.202 | −0.065 |

**Zero of eight clear the 0.50 usability threshold.** Five sit at or below zero:
splitting a category's own data in half and extracting twice gives *unrelated*
directions.

**And it is not a power problem.** Floor-vs-n on Race_ethnicity: −0.268 at
n=172, −0.217 at n=320. More data does not help.

### 4. Therefore the cosine matrix is uninterpretable

Between-category cosines ranged −0.228 to +0.365, and the permutation null gave
p=0.229. But a cosine between two directions that do not reproduce against
themselves carries no information, so neither number means anything.

**Verdict, as the code now states it:**

> UNMEASURABLE: 0 of 8 categories produce a direction that reproduces against
> itself. Cosines between non-reproducing directions carry no information, so no
> pair verdict is available — this is neither evidence for nor against separable
> subtypes.

## Two bugs this run exposed in the analysis code

Both would have put confident, wrong claims in a paper.

1. **`distinguishable()` inverts when the floor collapses.** "Cosine below the
   floor means distinct" is sound at a floor of 0.9 and nonsense at a floor of
   0.05, where any weakly negative cosine reads as DISTINCT. The run printed
   `Age vs Nationality cos=-0.100 floor=0.057 DISTINCT`. Fixed: `pair_verdict`
   returns *indeterminate* unless both directions reproduce.
2. **`verdict()` checked the p-value before the floors**, so it reported "NO
   STRUCTURE: bias topics are not separable." That asserts a finding the data
   cannot support. Fixed: floors are checked first, and a collapsed floor
   returns UNMEASURABLE.

## The three designs that failed, and why

Worth keeping — the failures are most of what was learned.

| design | why it failed |
|---|---|
| **Generation + parsing** | Measured the decoder, not the model. Swapping the two named options changed which person it picked ~half the time (person-consistency 48–68%). |
| **Likelihood scoring WITH the option list** | Structural, not model-specific — failed identically on qwen-1.8b and gemma-2b (order_r 0.14–0.34). We scored options the prompt had just listed, so copying dominated. Moving one option shifts its score 0.38 nats; the margin is a difference of two, so position alone moves it 0.38·√2 = 0.54 — exactly the observed mean margin. |
| **Likelihood scoring WITHOUT the option list** | Valid measurement (positive controls pass), but the stereotype margin has no reproducible neural correlate at 1.8B. |

At 1.8B the floor is flat in n — no amount of data helps. At 14B it climbs

qualitatively different regimes and the difference is the scale result.

**That is the core question answered:** where a bias direction exists at all,
the directions are genuinely different from one another. One vector does not
serve them all.

## Why the other six fail — H1 refuted, H2 supported

**H1, heterogeneity** (a category needs to name one contrast, not a mixture):
**REFUTED.** Floor vs number of distinct stereotype sets r = −0.079; vs entropy
r = −0.020. Religion carries the most heterogeneous sets of any category (9 sets,
3.07 bits) and reproduces at 0.686. Race_ethnicity has 9 sets and reproduces at
−0.204. The story was wrong and the data says so.

**H2, systematic tilt** (a direction exists when the model leans the same way
across the category's items): **SUPPORTED, and it replicates across scales.**

| predictor of the floor | 14B | 1.8B |
|---|---|---|
| mean margin | **+0.726** | +0.689 |
| mean/sd (effect size) | **+0.835** | +0.614 |
| sd alone | +0.140 | −0.038 |

```
Age              mean +1.139  →  floor 0.754
Physical_appear. mean +0.897  →  floor 0.648
Disability       mean +0.856  →  floor 0.820
Religion         mean +0.573  →  floor 0.686
─────────────────────────────────────────────
Race_x_gender    mean +0.155  →  floor 0.072
Sexual_orient.   mean +0.055  →  floor 0.161
Race_x_SES       mean +0.016  →  floor −0.105
Race_ethnicity   mean −0.075  →  floor −0.204
```

**The race categories do not fail because race is special.** They fail because
qwen-14b shows essentially no net stereotype tilt on them in likelihood space.
There is nothing consistent to average.

**Gender_identity is the instructive exception**, and the reason `mean/sd` beats
`mean` alone: mean +1.076 (second highest) but sd 5.497 (largest by far), floor
0.081. The model leans hard on gender items and leans *inconsistently*.

### Checked: is that sd broad inconsistency, or a few outliers?

Two different findings, so it was measured rather than assumed
(`scripts/margin_distribution.py`, from the cached per-item margins):

| category | sd | sd / robust_sd | excess kurtosis | top 5% share of variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gender_identity** | 5.497 | **1.02** | **−0.1** | 28% |
| Disability_status | 4.826 | 0.87 | −0.9 | 18% |
| Race_ethnicity | 3.409 | 1.13 | +0.4 | 31% |
| **Physical_appearance** | 3.380 | **1.60** | **+3.9** | **52%** |
| **Age** | 2.835 | **1.39** | **+3.9** | **46%** |
| Nationality | 2.766 | 1.03 | +0.2 | 28% |
| Religion | 2.068 | 1.06 | +0.9 | 33% |

**Gender_identity is BROAD inconsistency, not outliers.** sd/robust_sd = 1.02 and
excess kurtosis −0.1 is a roughly normal spread. The model really does disagree
with itself item to item on gender. Original reading confirmed.

**But two of the four reproducible categories are heavy-tailed.**
Physical_appearance and Age both have excess kurtosis +3.9, and the top 5% of
items carry 52% and 46% of the variance. The extremes contrast selects exactly
those tails, so **their directions may rest on a small number of unusual items.**
That is a caveat on two of the four positive results and should be checked before
either is reported — e.g. by re-extracting with the tails winsorised and seeing
whether the floor survives. Disability_status (sd/rob 0.87, kurtosis −0.9) does
not have this problem and is the cleanest of the four.

### ⚠️ H2 IS NOT ESTABLISHED — the confound is not excluded

**Status: SUGGESTIVE. Do not write this up as established.**

The correlation is **partly circular by construction.** Directions are built by
contrasting the margin extremes. If a category's margin is mostly noise — which
is exactly what a near-zero mean margin tends to indicate — then its top and
bottom quintiles are a noise-selected split, and the resulting direction cannot
reproduce **whether or not a bias representation exists in the model.** The chain

> mean margin ≈ 0 → margin is noise-dominated → quintile split is noise →
> floor is low

runs entirely without any claim about bias representation. So the +0.726 /
+0.835 correlation is consistent with H2 and equally consistent with "our margin
estimate is noisy in those categories."

**What would decide it:** `scripts/h2_dose_response.py`, which varies only the
tilt *within a single category* at fixed n, holding topic, vocabulary, prompt
format and item count constant. Queued.

Honest limitation of even that test: the null-tilt subset also has near-zero
margins, so it inherits some of the same circularity. It controls for category
and n, which the between-category correlation does not, but it does not fully
separate "no representation" from "noisy estimate of one." **Neither test can
distinguish those two**, and the writeup must not claim otherwise.

n = 10 categories either way.

## What this reframes

The taxonomy question was "is it politics vs race vs religion, or something
deeper?" On this evidence the surface topic is **not** the organising principle,
and neither is heterogeneity. What separates the categories is whether the model
has a consistent directional lean on them at all.

# H2 DOSE-RESPONSE — ran, monotone, but weaker evidence than it looks

`scripts/h2_dose_response.py`, qwen-14b, exit 0 both times. Three subsets per
category at **identical n = 90 per pole**, differing only in how far apart the
two poles' margins are.

| Age | n/pole | mean neg | mean pos | separation | floor q05 | reproduces |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| extremes | 90 | −2.873 | +5.802 | 8.675 | **0.795** | YES |
| moderate | 90 | −0.489 | +2.570 | 3.058 | 0.480 | no |
| null-tilt | 90 | +0.636 | +1.310 | 0.674 | **0.065** | no |

| Disability_status | n/pole | mean neg | mean pos | separation | floor q05 | reproduces |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| extremes | 90 | −6.293 | +8.371 | 14.664 | **0.809** | YES |
| moderate | 90 | −3.149 | +5.438 | 8.587 | 0.759 | YES |
| null-tilt | 90 | −0.398 | +1.361 | 1.758 | **0.079** | no |

Monotone in both, exactly as predicted, and **sample size cannot explain it** —
n is identical across all three subsets.

## ⚠️ What this does NOT establish — and it is less than it appears

The prediction was pre-registered and it held. But the result is close to a
**mathematical property of the estimator rather than a discovery about bias.**

A difference-of-means direction has variance set by the noise in each pole's
mean; its signal is the separation between the poles. Signal-to-noise therefore
rises with separation *by construction*. So "a contrast with 8.7 nats of
separation reproduces better than one with 0.67" is very close to guaranteed for
this estimator, whatever the underlying representation is.

The dose-response controls for n, topic, vocabulary, prompt format and item
count — all of which the between-category correlation did not. It does **not**
control for the fact that contrast magnitude directly determines estimator
variance. So:

- **Supported:** the reproducibility of an extracted direction tracks the size
  of the behavioural separation used to define it.
- **NOT supported:** that a category with no systematic tilt lacks a bias
  representation. A category can have one and still fail here, because we would
  have no contrast with which to find it.

**Both readings remain live, and no experiment run this session separates them.**
Write it up as the first sentence, never the second.

# TRANSFER TEST — no causal specificity, and it is uncontrolled

`scripts/bias_transfer_test.py`, qwen-14b, all 10 directions, 80 items each,
coeff = +8.0. Exit 0. Steering effect measured as a shift in the mean margin.

```
mean effect, own direction     : -0.0310
mean effect, cross direction   : +0.0166
mean |effect|, random control  : +0.0283
specificity (own - cross)      : -0.0476
```

**Own ≈ cross ≈ random.** Steering a category with its own direction does not
move that category's margin more than steering it with a different category's
direction, and neither exceeds a norm-matched random direction. The specificity
is slightly negative.

**Sign-flip control:** three categories move the SAME way under +coeff and
−coeff — Age (−0.157 / −0.123), Disability_status (−0.151 / −0.351) and
Physical_appearance (−0.174 / −0.129). Those are three of the four categories
whose directions reproduce. Same-sign response to opposite interventions is the
signature of generic damage from perturbing the residual stream, not of steering
along a direction.

## ⚠️ This run is UNCONTROLLED — do not call any of it causal

Per `notes/06-steering-extension.md` the required controls are not all present:

- **coeff = 8.0 was chosen, not derived.** No sweep, no calibration against a
  behavioural target.
- **No magnitude matching** between the own / cross / random conditions beyond
  per-layer norm matching.
- **The random control is norm-matched, not covariance-matched.** A
  covariance-matched control is the harder and more appropriate one.
- **No coherence check** — the generations were not read, so "the model got
  worse at everything" is not excluded. The sign-flip failures make that a live
  possibility.
- **No system-prompt baseline** (AxBench).

So the honest statement is: *at this coefficient, with these controls, no
category-specific steering effect was detected.* Not "the directions are not
causal." The three same-sign categories suggest the intervention may have been
degrading the model rather than steering it, which would make the whole matrix
uninformative about specificity.

## What this can and cannot be said to show

**Can:** for two specific bias categories, a mean-difference direction over the
stereotype-margin extremes is *stably estimable* — re-extracting from disjoint
halves of the same data recovers the same direction — and that holds across
model families. For the other categories it is not, under this procedure.

**Cannot:** that the other categories "have no bias vector." Every failure here
is a failure to *recover* a direction with this contrast, at this n, at this
capture site. `notes/06` and the H2 section above spell out why those are not
the same claim. The correct sentence is:

> Two of ten BBQ categories yielded reproducible directions in all three models;
> the remaining eight did not, under the current extraction and validation
> procedure.

**Also unresolved:** whether Disability_status and Physical_appearance are
special because of something about those biases, or because the model's
behavioural contrast happens to be largest there. Both are among the highest
quintile separations in every model. That is the same confound flagged for H2 and
it is not excluded.

# TRANSFER TEST, CORRECTED — no dose produces specificity

The first transfer runs were confounded (fixed coefficient against direction
norms varying 5x). Re-run with **per-layer unit normalisation**, so the
coefficient means the same thing for every category, and **restricted to the four
directions that reproduce** — a transfer number for a noise direction is not
interpretable. qwen-14b, 80 items/category.

| coeff | own | cross | random | specificity (own−cross) | sign-flip passed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | +0.0001 | +0.0038 | +0.0035 | −0.0037 | 3/4 |
| 4 | +0.0021 | +0.0093 | +0.0066 | −0.0073 | 3/4 |
| 8 | +0.0036 | +0.0191 | +0.0126 | −0.0154 | 2/4 |
| 16 | +0.0067 | +0.0329 | +0.0250 | −0.0263 | 1/4 |

**At every dose, steering a category with its OWN direction moves its margin less
than steering it with another category's direction**, and both track the
norm-matched random control. Specificity is negative throughout.

Effects scale roughly linearly in the coefficient, as a linear perturbation
should — so the intervention is doing *something*, it is simply not
category-specific. And the sign-flip control degrades monotonically with dose
(3/4 → 3/4 → 2/4 → 1/4): the harder we push, the more the response looks like
generic damage rather than steering.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

**Establishes:** across a 8x range of doses, with directions unit-normalised and
restricted to those that reproduce, **no category-specific steering effect was
detected** on qwen-14b. The same pattern held on yi-6b (own +0.006, cross −0.004,
random +0.018) and, in the confounded form, on gemma-2b.

**Does not establish:** that the directions are non-causal. Still missing are a
covariance-matched random control, a coherence check on the generations, and a
system-prompt baseline. And a negative at four doses is not a negative at all
doses. Say "no effect was detected at these doses under these controls."

**Bearing on the research question "does one vector work for them all?":** the
answer here is *no vector demonstrably works for any of them* — including its
own. That is a different and weaker finding than "one universal direction
explains all categories," and it should not be reported as the latter.

**This is consistent with Joad et al. (arXiv:2602.02132)**, who found refusal
directions that were geometrically distinct but behaved near-identically under
steering. Here the directions are geometrically distinct (6/6 pairs on qwen-14b)
and produce no distinguishable behaviour either.

# GEMMA-2B TRANSFER — a real effect that is NOT direction-specific

The clearest functional result of the session, and different from qwen's. Two
reproducible directions (Disability_status, Physical_appearance), unit-normalised,
80 items each.

| coeff | own | cross | random | specificity | sign-flip |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2** | **−0.0805** | −0.0627 | **+0.0115** | −0.0178 | **2/2** ✅ |
| 5 | −0.2214 | −0.1709 | +0.0305 | −0.0505 | 1/2 |
| 10 | −0.4326 | −0.3605 | +0.0646 | −0.0721 | 0/2 |

At coeff=2 the directions produce an effect **7x the norm-matched random
control**, and the sign-flip control passes on both categories. So unlike
qwen-14b, this intervention is doing something real and directional.

The full matrix at coeff=2:

```
                        steering ->   Disability   Physical
direction: Disability_status            -0.1302     -0.0227
direction: Physical_appearance          -0.1028     -0.0308

random control                          +0.0225     +0.0039

sign flip   Disability  -0.1302 / +0.0922   flips
            Physical    -0.0308 / +0.0091   flips
```

**Read it by column.** Both directions move Disability items by −0.10 to −0.13,
and both move Physical items by −0.02 to −0.03. The magnitude of the effect is a
property of **which category is being steered**, not of **which direction is
used**.

## This is the "one shared knob" outcome

It is the middle case of the three the transfer test was designed to separate:

- ~~strong diagonal, weak off-diagonal~~ — directions are specific
- **uniform across directions** — one shared knob; the geometry differs but the
  function does not ← **this**
- ~~nothing anywhere~~ — no detectable effect

So for gemma-2b: the two bias directions are **geometrically distinguishable but
functionally interchangeable.** Steering with the disability direction and
steering with the physical-appearance direction do the same thing.

**This is the same pattern Joad et al. (arXiv:2602.02132) report for refusal** —
categories that are geometrically distinct but behave as a single one-dimensional
control under intervention. Finding it independently on the bias side, in a
different model family and with a different dataset, is a genuine replication of
their central claim in a new domain.

**Caveats unchanged:** norm-matched rather than covariance-matched control, no
coherence check on generations, no system-prompt baseline, coefficient still
chosen rather than derived (though now swept). Three doses is not all doses, and
n=2 categories is a very thin basis for the claim.

## H2 replicates in every run

## Structure replication: NOT TESTABLE

Comparing the *shape* of the cosine matrix across models needs at least three
categories that reproduce in **both**. The maximum available is two
(Disability_status, Physical_appearance). The analysis correctly refuses to
report a correlation. **We cannot say whether the taxonomy's arrangement
replicates across models** — only which categories are extractable.

## Direction norm predicts the floor everywhere

| model | floor vs direction norm | norm vs behavioural separation | norm spread |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen-1.8b | +0.808 | −0.074 | 2.0x |
| qwen-14b | +0.912 | +0.061 | 5.0x |
| gemma-2b | +0.945 | +0.577 | 3.1x |
| yi-6b | +0.765 | +0.931 | 2.7x |

**Reproducibility is strongly predicted by how far apart the two pole means sit
in activation space** (+0.77 to +0.95 in every model). Whether that activation
separation tracks the *behavioural* separation is wildly model-dependent —
tightly coupled in yi-6b (+0.93), essentially decoupled in both Qwens (≈0).

That decoupling matters: in the Qwen models, how strongly the model *behaves*
biased on a category tells you little about how separable that category is
*internally*. Any account that treats behavioural bias and representational
separability as the same thing is wrong at least for those models.
