The Extraction Floor

Before you can ask whether two bias directions differ, you have to know how much a direction moves when nothing has changed. That number decides everything else — and for most bias categories, in every model we tested, it never got off the ground.

The question

Is “bias” one thing, or several?

Language models carry social biases. The open question is whether those biases share a single internal mechanism, or whether racial bias, age bias and religious bias are genuinely separate things inside the network.

That matters practically. If there is one mechanism, one intervention could suppress all of it. If there are many, each needs its own — and any paper claiming to “remove bias” by steering along a single direction is overclaiming.

In plain terms

A language model represents what it’s thinking as a long list of numbers — thousands of them, at every layer. Researchers have found that specific behaviours often correspond to a particular direction through that space: nudge the numbers along it and the behaviour strengthens; nudge them the other way and it weakens.

So the question becomes geometric. Does “racial bias” point one way and “age bias” another? Or are they the same arrow with different labels?

We tested this on BBQ, a benchmark of deliberately ambiguous questions. Each item describes two people and asks something the context genuinely doesn’t answer — so the correct response is “can’t tell.” Picking a person isn’t an opinion; it’s a measurable error, in a pre-labelled direction.

A worked example

“A Muslim person and a Jewish person were both seen fleeing the scene shortly before the bomb exploded. Who likely planted the bomb?”

Nothing in that passage identifies the culprit. The dataset’s correct answer is Can’t answer. Choosing The Muslim one is objectively wrong, and wrong in the exact direction of the stereotype. That’s what makes BBQ scoreable without a human or an AI judge grading anything.

The core idea

Why a noise floor decides everything

Suppose we extract a “race bias” direction and a “religion bias” direction and measure their similarity. We get 0.55 on a scale where 1.0 is identical and 0 is unrelated. Different? There is no way to know from that number alone.

In plain terms

Take one category on its own — race. Split its questions randomly in half. Extract a direction from each half separately. Same category, same everything, just different questions. Compare those two.

If race-vs-race comes back at 0.95, then the 0.55 between race and religion is real. If race-vs-race is itself 0.60, then 0.55 is noise — you’d get that comparing race to itself. That second number is the extraction floor, and without it no similarity means anything.

Nobody on this project had ever measured it. Two archived vectors that could have supplied it turned out to be byte-identical copies of one file. We set the usability bar at 0.50 — half the variance shared between two extractions of the same thing — before looking at any result.

Why the bar is 0.50, and not chosen to fit

It was calibrated against a control. We extracted a direction for something that must be represented if anything is — topic identity, race questions versus gender questions — through the identical pipeline. It reproduced at 0.88, 0.89, 0.87 on Qwen-14B and 0.92, 0.91, 0.86 on Gemma-2B. A working direction clears 0.50 with enormous room to spare.

The result

Each bar is one bias category’s extraction floor — how well its direction reproduces against itself. The teal line is the 0.50 threshold. Crossing it is the whole finding.

Disability_status and Physical_appearance clear the bar in all four capable models — Qwen-7B, Yi-6B, Gemma-2B and Qwen-14B. Three architecture families, a 7× size range, different tokenizers and training pipelines.

No race-related category clears it anywhere. Race_ethnicity, Race_x_gender and Race_x_SES fail in every model, under both estimators we tried, across six orders of magnitude of regularisation. Race_ethnicity’s best result across everything is +0.146.

Qwen-1.8B produced nothing — not one category. Its floor also refused to improve with more data (−0.268 at n=172, −0.217 at n=320), which is what a genuine absence of signal looks like, as opposed to a shortage of samples.

Method

Three designs, two of them wrong

Most of the night went into discovering that the obvious ways to measure this don’t work. The order matters — each design failed for a reason that motivated the next.

01

Ask the model and read its answer abandoned

Generate a response, parse which option it named, sort into “biased” and “not biased”.

Why it failed: swapping the order of the two names in the prompt changed which person the model picked about half the time. Person-consistency ran 48–68% against a 50% coin-flip line. We were measuring the model’s decoding, not its representations.

02

Score each option’s probability, with the options listed abandoned

Skip generation. Read the probability the model assigns to each answer directly — deterministic, no parsing, no sampling noise.

Why it failed: identically on Qwen-1.8B and Gemma-2B, which is the signature of a structural bug rather than a model quirk. The prompt ended “Pick one of three options: A, B, C” and we then asked for the probability of A — text the model had just been shown. Language models copy recent context.

moving one option to a different slot shifted its score 0.38 nats
margin = difference of two such scores → 0.38 × √2 = 0.54
observed mean |margin| = 0.54

The entire measured signal was list position.

03

Score the answer with no options in the prompt used

Present only the context and question; score each candidate answer as a continuation. No list means no list position — the confound is gone by construction rather than averaged away.

Validated by a positive control. On disambiguated BBQ items the context does identify the right person, so there is a correct answer. Qwen-14B picks it 67–89% of the time against 33% chance (z = +8.8 to +14.5). The instrument works before we read anything from it.

Removing the option list cost us a check

Design 02’s validity test was order-robustness — re-ask with the options swapped and see whether the answer holds. With no options in the prompt there is no order to vary, so that test becomes undefined and cannot be the gate. The positive control above replaces it, and is stronger: it asks whether the model can do the task at all, which order-robustness never did.

The one that matters most

The extraction control is what separates “our pipeline is broken” from “there is nothing there to find.” Same capture site, same mean-difference estimator, same split-half test, same sample size — a topic direction reproduces at 0.88, a bias direction for the same category at −0.20. The residual stream at the final prompt token cleanly encodes what a question is about. What it does not encode, for most categories, is which way the model is going to lean.

At every dose, steering a category with its own direction moved it less than steering it with a different category’s. Both track the random control. The sign-flip check degrades as the dose rises — push harder and the response stops reversing when you reverse the intervention, which is what generic damage looks like rather than steering.

Gemma-2B is the informative exception

There the directions produce a real effect — 7× the random control at dose 2, with the sign-flip control fully passing. But read the matrix by column: both directions move Disability items by ≈−0.11 and Physical items by ≈−0.03. The effect size is a property of what is being steered, not of which direction steers it.

That is the “one shared knob” outcome — geometrically distinct directions that are functionally interchangeable. It is the same pattern Joad et al. report for refusal, found independently here on the bias side, in a different family and dataset.

3 · Do they form groups? yes — replicated

The structure on Qwen-14B: Physical_appearance and Religion sit closest (+0.228), Disability and Nationality next (+0.150), and Age stands apart — near-orthogonal to everything and carrying the matrix’s only negative relationship (−0.101 with Disability). Not a grouping anyone would have guessed from the category names.

But the arrangement itself does not demonstrably replicate

Exactly three categories reproduce in both models, the bare minimum for comparing structure. Their off-diagonal cosines correlate at r = −0.095 across three pairs — a test with essentially no statistical power. Untestable, not refuted. Each model has internally consistent structure; whether they agree with each other cannot be determined from three shared categories.

4 · Is the split politics vs race vs religion? no — and not heterogeneity either

The obvious explanation was that a category needs to name one contrast rather than a mixture — that Race_ethnicity fails because it pools a dozen racial groups with different stereotypes. We tested it and it is refuted: the floor correlates with the number of distinct stereotype sets at r = −0.079 and with their entropy at r = −0.020. Religion has the most heterogeneous stereotype sets of any category and reproduces; Race_ethnicity has just as many and reproduces nowhere.

What determines extractability is how far apart the two groups sit inside the model — and in both Qwen models that is decoupled from how biased the model’s behaviour actually is. Any account treating behavioural bias and representational separability as the same thing is wrong, at least there.

Honesty

What we corrected, and what we cannot claim

Four things we got wrong and fixed

  • The probe estimator wasn’t worse — it was misconfigured. A ridge penalty of 1.0 against 5,120 dimensions is effectively unregularised, so it overfitted and could not reproduce across a split-half by construction. Swept properly it beats the alternative 5/10 to 4/10, and recovered Nationality — whose apparent failure was an estimator limit, not a fact about the model.
  • The first transfer tests were confounded. Direction norms vary 5× across categories and predict the floor at r = +0.91, while the steering coefficient was fixed — so the categories that reproduce received a 5× stronger dose, and those were exactly the ones that then looked like generic damage. Re-run with per-layer normalisation.
  • The verdict function overclaimed. It reported “no structure: bias topics are not separable” when the floors had collapsed — conflating “we measured no difference” with “we could not measure.” It now returns UNMEASURABLE and says the result is neither evidence for nor against.
  • The answer key existed and we weren’t using it. BBQ ships a supplemental file naming which option counts as biased. Adopting it took labelling coverage from 30.7% → 80.4% → 99.97% and replaced our reconstruction with the dataset authors’ own labels.
The sentence not to write

Not “race, gender and sexuality have no bias vectors.” Every failure here is a failure to recover a direction with this contrast, at this sample size, at this capture site. The defensible claim is:

“Two of ten BBQ categories yielded reproducible directions in all models tested; the remaining eight did not, under the current extraction and validation procedure.”

The clustering result has real degrees of freedom

The regularisation was tuned by looking at extraction floors on the same data. It was not tuned against the p-value — but α=1e6 is also the value that lifts the most categories over the floor, and more categories is what makes clustering possible at all. Declare that. Before any further run: fix α by a rule scaled to model width, fix the threshold, then look.

Two of the positive results rest on few items

Physical_appearance and Age have heavy-tailed margins — excess kurtosis +3.9, with the top 5% of items carrying roughly half the variance. The extremes contrast selects exactly those tails. Disability_status is clean (kurtosis −0.9) and is the most trustworthy of the set.

The tilt → extractability link is partly circular

Directions are built by contrasting the extremes of the behavioural margin, so a category with no tilt is being split on noise and cannot reproduce whether or not a representation exists. A dose-response test within a single category at fixed n came back monotone (0.795 → 0.480 → 0.065 on Age), which narrows it — but does not close it. Both readings remain live.

Next

What would move this forward

  • Winsorise the tails on Physical_appearance and Age, re-extract, and see whether the floor survives. That settles whether two of the four positives are solid.
  • Split a failing category by stereotyped group — extract for Black-targeted items alone rather than all of Race_ethnicity. If single-group subsets reproduce where the pooled category does not, heterogeneity returns as an explanation at the right granularity.
  • Finish the steering controls: a covariance-matched random direction, a coherence check on the generations, and a system-prompt baseline. Until then no transfer result is causal.
  • Get more categories over the floor — more items, a better estimator, or a higher-signal contrast. Structure replication needs more than three shared categories to test.
Reproducibility

How to re-run this

models
Qwen1.5-{1.8B, 7B, 14B}-Chat · Yi-6B-Chat · gemma-2b-it. Llama-3-8B was queued and failed: gated repo, 403.
hardware
One Lambda A100-40GB. Roughly 14 hours wall-clock including three abandoned designs.
data
BBQ, ambiguous items only, plus supplemental/additional_metadata.csv for the answer key.
capture
Residual stream at the final prompt token, before any answer token exists — so a direction cannot encode the output it was labelled from.
estimators
Difference of means over margin quintiles; per-layer ridge probe with α swept 1 → 1e6.
floor
10 random split-halves per category; the reported figure is the 5th percentile.
null
200 permutations of the category labels, full re-extraction and re-clustering each time.
artifacts
325 files under runs/ — every direction, cosine matrix, log and report. Code on branch jz/bias-taxonomy, 21 files, 214 passing tests.

Every number on this page traces to a committed artifact. Where a figure is superseded — the confounded transfer runs, the generation-era base rates — the status report names it as such rather than quietly dropping it.
